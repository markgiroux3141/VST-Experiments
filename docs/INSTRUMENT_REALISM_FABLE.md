# Instrument realism: the Fable review of the tube synth and the Rhodes Suitcase

2026-11-13, at the user's request: a read-only review by Claude Fable 5 of the tube synth
(`tube_synth`) and the Rhodes Suitcase (`rhodes_suitcase`), asking where each falls short of how
a real analog instrument reacts to being played, and which simplifications cost sound quality or
realism. It is written as work packages so that a later session on any model (Opus included) can
build each one without having been in the conversation: nine for the Suitcase (SC1-SC9), four
for the synth (TS1-TS4), one for both (PL1).

**Nothing here is built and nothing was run.** No model, circuit, engine or app code changed.
What was read: RHODES_PLAN.md, RHODES_VARIATION.md, TUBE_SYNTH_PLAN.md and SYNTH_MASTER_PLAN.md
in full; the headings of SYNTH_IDEAS.md and COMPONENT_DEPTH_PLAN.md; `config/rhodes/mk1.yaml`;
the headers of `tube_synth.yaml`, `rhodes_suitcase.yaml` and parts of `rhodes_suitcase_100w.yaml`;
`varimu/rhodes/compile.py`, parts of `spec.py` and `harp.py`; circuitsim's `harp.rs` (module
docs, the voice's events); varimu-app's `panel.rs` (the two devices), `live.rs` (`build`),
`performance.rs` (module docs); varimu-circuit's `speaker.rs` (`readout_apart`);
`varimu/clavinet/wear.py`; `varimu/devices/contact.py` and `noise.py` (names);
`varimu/circuit/builder.py` (`_noise_elements`) and `parts.py` (`opamp`, `speaker_driver`);
`scripts/rhodes_vs_jrhodes.py`; `tests/test_tube_synth.py` (`run`). No web search.

Tags on claims:
- **[code]**: read in the code on 2026-11-13; the path (and line) is given. Line numbers drift:
  when one is off, search for the name.

## Short answer

Both instruments are careful physics from the pickup or the oscillator onward. The gaps are at
the edges: what the player touches, what varies from key to key and unit to unit, and what the
live tier leaves out that the render has. In the order I would work them:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SC1 the speakers heard live | Suitcase | app | a convolution |
| 2 | SC2 every key its own (unit draws) | Suitcase | reference + engine | none |

## Rules for whoever builds this

These are the project's rules (CLAUDE.md) as they apply here. Follow them without being asked.

   write the options with numbers into this file's package (a "Status" block under it, dated)
   and ask. Do not refit a device, change a circuit's values or adopt a variant on your own.

4. **Every value has a source**: a datasheet or manual reference, `web: <url>`, `fit: ...` with
   what it was fitted to, or `provisional: model knowledge (...)`. Every **[memory]** claim in

   (`scripts/compile_rhodes_harp.py`), and moving a circuit to it is an explicit edit of the

   `varimu/circuit/builder.py` and `crates/varimu-circuit` alike, absent = unchanged IR (so
   `scripts/export_circuit_fixtures.py --golden` shows no diff), then
   `scripts/export_app_fixtures.py` and `cargo test -p varimu-circuit --test agreement`.
8. **The render cache.** `varimu/rhodes`, `varimu/synth` and `varimu/clavinet` are outside the

   need a core or device variant put it in a standalone module first (as

9. **Rust edits don't move the caches.** After a circuitsim edit use `uv run --extra fast ...`

10. **Reproducible.** Python-only randomness through `np.random.default_rng([seed,

12. **Tests.** Per package run only its own tests. The full suite and `slice_report.py --verify`

13. **Estimate a run's time before starting it**, from the rates in "What exists", and tell the

14. **Benchmarks** on this desktop drift +-35%: wait until no `rustc` runs, take the best of

15. **Nothing goes into the web app** (`varimu listen`). Do not write patch scripts as shell

## What exists (the facts every package leans on)

### The Rhodes Suitcase

- **The chain** [docs: RHODES_PLAN.md]: MIDI velocity -> hammer speed (one law for all keys: 5.0
  m/s at 127 over 21 dB, [code: `config/rhodes/mk1.yaml` `velocity`]) -> a Hunt-Crossley contact
  on the tine -> the tone bar assembly's complex modes (one plane) -> the pickup's flux map (a
  3D solve, one map for all keys) -> the harp's wiring -> the preamp (`rhodes_suitcase.yaml`,
  fig11-1) -> the LED / photocell vibrato -> the dual power amp on its mains supply -> four
  12 in drivers (`rhodes_suitcase_100w.yaml`).
- **The reference** is Python: `varimu/rhodes/` (`spec.py` builds a key with its fits,
  `beam.py` the FE, `key.py` a strike, `harp.py` voices over events, `pickup.py` /
  `pickup3d.py` the map, `compile.py` the component). **The live medium** is circuitsim's
  `harp.rs`, playing the component `MK1_HARP@2` (`config/components/mk1_harp/2.yaml` pins
  `2.harp.json`). Agreement: `tests/test_rhodes_compiled.py`, `crates/circuitsim/tests/harp.rs`.
- **What the component holds** [code: `varimu/rhodes/compile.py:117-207`]: per key `weight`,
  `hammer` (mass, stiffness, exponent, hysteresis), the `free` and `damped` systems (modes
  `lam`, input `b`, outputs `strike`, `tip`, the reduced `arc`), the two transfers between
  them; once for all keys the flux map, **one `voicing_m`** (line 195), the velocity law, the
  contact window, `sleep_m`, the coils.
- **A voice's events** [code: `crates/circuitsim/src/harp.rs:733-767`]: `On` throws the hammer
  at the tine wherever it is; `Off` and `Pedal` call `settle_damper`, an instant switch between
  the free and the damped system (`switch`, line ~690); `Pressure` is ignored. The pedal is a
  bool. A sleeping voice's modes are zeroed at the next strike (line 738).
- **The damper** [code: `varimu/rhodes/spec.py:231` `_fit_damper`]: a dashpot at half the tine,
  fitted per key so the released fundamental's T60 is `damper.t60_s` = 0.15 s for every key
  (`mk1.yaml:62`, provisional).
- **Tuning**: every key within 0.002 cent of equal temperament [docs: RH2's survey]. **Voicing**:
  2.0 mm for every key, where the fit to the real piano wanted 0.9-2.4 mm per key
  [code: `mk1.yaml:93`].
- **Live** [code: `crates/varimu-app/src/panel.rs:1157-1204`]: draft-2x, five partition threads,
  Newton (4e-3, 4e-2), and **`live_speaker_v: Some(14.0)`: the app plays each channel's
  speaker terminal voltage, not its sound pressure**. `live::Circuit::build`
  [code: `live.rs:224-264`] builds the readouts only when `live_speaker_v` is None, and then
  splits each `<PART> readout` the partition names (`varimu_circuit::speaker::readout_apart`).
  The speaker parts are `ASPK` and `BSPK` (sections "speakers A" / "speakers B", `series: 2`)
  [code: `rhodes_suitcase_100w.yaml:456-473`].
- **Noise**: neither Suitcase file has a `noise:` block. The builder's noise covers resistors
  (R, pot, rheostat, t_pad, windings) and tubes only [code: `builder.py:358-408`]; BJTs, diodes
  and the `opamp` macro have none. An op-amp's voltage noise is written as a resistor at its
  input, heard only under a noise block (the pattern: `config/circuits/clean_pre.yaml`, `RN` /
  `u_noise_ohm`).
- **Real references** (untracked, never commit): `real instrument samples/Rhodes Mk I 1978 -
  with Suitcase preamp by Tines and reeds.mp3` (chords, the preamp's output) and
  `real instrument samples/jrhodes3d_download/jlearman.jRhodes3d-master/jRhodes3d-mono/`
  (a 1977 Mk I Stage 73 **recorded from the harp connector**: 15 notes x up to 5 layers, up to
  25 s; EQ'd, noise-reduced, level-normalized: trust decays, frequencies, pitch, layer-to-layer
  changes). `scripts/rhodes_vs_jrhodes.py` loads them and renders ours (`harp_note`:
  `circuitsim.harp_render` on the compiled model; `harmonics_db`, `decay_db_s`, `matched`).
- **Rates** [docs]: the harp alone, all 73 keys ringing, 0.22x real time; the preamp-only
  circuit ~4x real time at production; the whole Suitcase at draft ~25x with the two readouts,
  at production 158-455x (don't render the whole instrument at production without asking);
  live 0.75-0.81x on a loud program.
- **Scripts**: `rhodes_render.py (--midi F | --demo) [--circuit rhodes_suitcase]`,
  `rhodes_suitcase_notes.py`, `suitcase_bands.py`, `suitcase_study.py`, `rhodes_amp_study.py`,
  `rhodes_key_study.py`, `rhodes_keyboard_survey.py`, `rhodes_fit_keys.py`,

  `test_rhodes_compiled.py`, `test_rhodes_suitcase.py`, `test_rhodes_amp.py`,

### The tube synth

- **The circuit** `config/circuits/tube_synth.yaml` (its 175-line header describes every
  block): a reactor-tuned LC oscillator and a gas-tube saw, a saturable-reactor low-pass with
  a Q multiplier, an ADSR, a push-pull 6386 VCA into an output transformer, a lamp-held Wien
  LFO, a selenium ring modulator, all on one 6X4 supply with 5651 references. Control inputs
  `gate`, `pitch`, `key`, `pressure`, `pitch2` [code: lines 178-183].
- **It has** `noise: {seed: 1}` and `heaters: {tap: heater}` [code: lines 193-201] and **no
  `tolerance:` block** (searched: none). varimu-circuit refuses a nonzero tolerance spread
  [code: `crates/varimu-circuit/src/builder.rs:8-10`]. Python's draws are
  `np.random.default_rng([seed, crc32(designator)])` [code: `varimu/circuit/parts.py:1057`].
- **The cores** are Jiles-Atherton (`varimu/devices/core.py`, circuitsim `devices/core.rs`),
  live too (`cores: Some("ja")`). The continuous Preisach ensemble exists in Python
  (`varimu/devices/preisach.py`: `PreisachParams`, `Preisach`) and in circuitsim only inside
  the tape (`tape.rs`, `tape_stream.rs`): there is no Preisach core device.
- **The keyboard is the app's** [code: `crates/varimu-app/src/performance.rs:21-25`]: the last
  key held sounds, a legato key lifts the gate for `RETRIGGER_GAP_S` (1 ms), velocity sets the
  bus's key-down volts from a table, the pitch input holds the last key's volts. In the circuit
  the pitch string is a stiff source into RPSUM / GLIDE / a stopper [code: yaml section
  "keyboard", lines 898-931]. The `contact` device kind is ideal: "no bounce, no arc, no wiping
  resistance" [code: `varimu/devices/contact.py`].
- **Live** [code: `panel.rs:786-830`]: draft-2x, up to six partition threads, noise off by
  default, the saw on event kernels.
- **Measured behaviour** [docs: TUBE_SYNTH_PLAN.md]: a key change settles within 10 cents in
  17-45 ms, big drops 0.1-0.24 s; a held note droops ~19 cents; mains 5.5 cents per volt; the
  mains wander 1.8 cents rms over 10 s; a cold start is within half a cent by ~40 s; the gate's
  thump 1.3 mV; hum with a key held -71 dBFS; in the reactor's control circuit the odd
  harmonics sit 134 dB under the second.
- **Playing a note in Python** [code: `tests/test_tube_synth.py:59-83`]:

  ```python

  ir = builder.build_ir(s, spec.resolve(s, {"cutoff": 0.5}), cores="ja")   # overrides: a dict
  st = dataclasses.replace(

      "gate": gate, "pitch": np.full(n, pitch_v), "key": np.zeros(n),
      "pressure": np.zeros(n), "pitch2": np.zeros(n)}).vectors            # every control, always
  ```

  `KEY_UP_V, KEY_DOWN_V = -110.0, -1.0`; -15 V on `pitch` is ~497 Hz. Readings (cycle
  frequencies, glide, jitter) are in `varimu/synth/vco.py`.
- **Rates** [docs]: draft with the app's partition ~0.9x real time on the bench; the oscillator
  alone 3.4x at production; the whole synth at production has never been measured.

# The Rhodes Suitcase

## SC1. The speakers heard live

**The gap.** Live, `out` / `out_r` are the speakers' terminal voltages [code: `panel.rs:1201`].
Renders are the pressure 1 m in front of each channel's front driver. By RHODES_PLAN.md's band
table the speakers add, on top of the preamp's output, +7.6 dB at 1-2 kHz, +13.3 dB at 2-4 kHz,
+6 dB at 4-8 kHz and -12.5 dB at 8-16 kHz [docs]. So the instrument the user plays and the
instrument a take renders differ most where the bark is. `rhodes_stage_5e3` has the same gap
(`live_speaker_v: Some(20.0)`, `panel.rs:1153`).

**The idea.** With a linear driver, the pressure is one fixed linear filter of the terminal
voltage: the voltage sets the coil current, the cone's motion and the radiated pressure,
whatever the amp's output impedance is. So the app can keep the circuit as it runs today (the
speakers as loads) and apply the speaker's transfer after the device, with no extra partition
thread. That matters because the Suitcase's live problem is thread scheduling, not compute
[docs]. [estimate: the filter's impulse response is tens of milliseconds at most (the driver's
resonance ringing, the back wave's delay round the cabinet).]

**Steps.**
1. **Check the premise** [Python]. Confirm `parts.speaker_driver` / `speaker_readout` expand to
   linear elements for `P12R@1` without a `field` (R, L, C, Vccs only). If anything is
   nonlinear, stop and use the alternative below.
2. **Measure H** [Python, `scripts/suitcase_speaker_filter.py`]: a small circuit with one
   speaker part exactly as `ASPK` (same component, `series: 2`, cabinet values, distance)
   driven by an ideal voltage source; its impulse response from the terminal voltage to `out`
   at the live rate (96 kHz), by a transient run of an impulse or by `varimu/measure/ac.py`
   (AC analysis of linear IRs). Save it to `config/speaker_filters/<circuit>.json` with the
   sha256 of the circuit file and the component version it was measured on.
3. **Verify** [Python]: render a short note set through `rhodes_suitcase_100w` at draft twice,
   with the readouts (`build_ir(..., speaker_readout=True)`, the default) and without; convolve
   the second with H; the difference against the first re the signal.
4. **The app**: a device option (e.g. `panel::Device::speaker_filter`) that convolves each
   channel after the device and before the monitor, the full scale then the file's
   `output_pascals_peak` (25 Pa). circuitsim has a convolver (`circuitsim::convolve`, the
   plate's): check that it adds no latency, or use a direct FIR for the head. A stale filter
   (hash mismatch) is refused, as stale reductions are.
5. **A monitor choice** on the Play panel: DI (the preamp's outputs, what the reference
   recording is) | SPEAKERS. DI needs the probes `pre_a` / its right-channel twin kept in the
   live build (`live::live_probes`).

**The alternative** (the 5E3's route since 2026-11-12): `live_speaker_v: None` and
`"ASPK readout"`, `"BSPK readout"` as partition groups [code: `panel.rs:669-676` for the 5E3].
It adds two threads and the front wave's states to the speakers' threads; measure with
`examples/play.rs --device rhodes_suitcase --strike-all 127 --restrike 3 --mute` before and
after.

**Tests.** The filter's agreement with the readout build (a bound a few dB outside the measured
value); a stale filter refused; the device's stereo channels each filtered.

**Done when** the live device with SPEAKERS agrees with the readout build within the tested
bound on the note set, the load and dropouts are reported back to back with today's, and the
user has heard DI against SPEAKERS.

**Cost.** Step 3's renders: ~10 s of audio at ~25x real time, twice: ~8 min [estimate].

**The user's.** Which of the two the device plays by default. Note that `P12R@1` is a stand-in
for unknown drivers, so SPEAKERS is a provisional curve heard loudly: SC9 is its follow-up.

## SC2. Every key its own: unit draws for the harp

**The gap.** All 73 keys share one voicing, one gap, exact tuning and nominal tips. A real
Mark I is uneven from key to key, and its chords beat a little. The real piano the harp was
fitted to wanted a voicing of 0.9-2.4 mm per key [code: `mk1.yaml:93`], and its notes sit
within ~3 cents of equal temperament from E2 up [docs]. That spread is measured, not invented.

**The pattern** is the Clavinet's wear: `varimu/clavinet/wear.py` (the reference: a seed, an
age, shares; every draw a pure function of seed, name and key), circuitsim's `ClavModel::worn`
(applied once at load, no cost per sample), the IR's optional `clav_strings.wear`, the app's
UNIT # and AGE [docs: CLAVINET_PLAN.md "Variation and wear"]. Read that module's docstring
first: it says which changes are safe at load and which broke the contact.

**What can change at load** (the voices run the same operations on other numbers):
- **voicing**: a per-key offset added to `voicing_m` where the pickup is read
  [code: `harp.rs` `render`, `free_run_impl`: `flux.gradient(voicing + tip, arc, ...)`];
- **the gap**: a per-key constant added to the arc's pull-back, within the flux map's
  `dg_range_m` (a smaller gap than the compiled one is outside the map: the draw is one-sided
  or the map is recomputed wider, the builder's call after reading `pickup3d.py`);
- **tip hardness**: `hammer.stiffness` scaled per key (Shore A through `spec.gent_youngs_pa`,
  as the Clavinet's tangents);
- **tuning**: each key's `lam` scaled by a ratio in the free and damped systems. This is the
  delicate one: the contact steps use the compiled modes. Check a detuned key against the
  Python reference rebuilt with a changed spring mass (`spec._spring_mass` tunes a key) before
  trusting it, and read how `contact_step` is built in `harp.rs`.

**Steps.**
1. **Measure the spreads** on jRhodes3d (`scripts/rhodes_unit_spread.py`): each note's pitch
   offset in cents (its fundamental against equal temperament at A440, the layers' median) and,
   if the per-key voicing fit can be re-run (it was a scratch fit; RHODES_PLAN.md "Applied
   (2026-11-07)" describes it), each note's voicing. 15 notes give a standard deviation with a
   wide error bar: say so in the source string.
2. **The reference** `varimu/rhodes/unit.py`: `Unit(seed, amount)`, amount 0 = the compiled
   harp exactly, 1 = the measured spreads, at most 2; `apply(model, unit) -> model`.
3. **The port**: `HarpModel::with_unit` in `harp.rs`; the IR's optional `harp.unit` (absent =
   today's IR); the part's optional params in both builders.
4. **The app**: UNIT # and EVENNESS (or the Clavinet's names) on `rhodes_suitcase` and
   `rhodes_stage_5e3`; a change restarts the stream.
5. **Listen**: a chord progression and a chromatic run at amount 0 / 1 / 2, loudness-matched
   (`scripts/rhodes_unit_demo.py` -> `reports/rhodes/unit/`).

**Tests** (`tests/test_rhodes_unit.py`, `crates/circuitsim/tests/harp.rs`): amount 0 is the
compiled model bit for bit; Rust equals Python for the same seed; one key's draw doesn't move
when another amount changes; a detuned key reads its drawn cents; the builders agree.

**Done when** a unit plays live at no measurable extra load, amount 0 is bit for bit today's
harp, and the user has heard 0 against 1.

**Not in this package:** per-strike variation (SC7) and age (worn felts, hardened grommets:
RH6's dials, which need a recompile).

## SC3. The release as a contact

**The gap.** A release is an instant switch to a damped system with one T60 for every key, and
a key's release velocity and the pedal's position are ignored [code: `harp.rs:755-763`,
`spec.py:231`]. The user heard it: "almost the identical key attack and liftoff"
[docs: RHODES_VARIATION.md, which parks the fix as its item 1]. On a real piano the felt lands
on a moving tine: a large swing leaves the felt and meets it again [memory], and the bass takes
longer to stop than the treble [memory].

**Steps.**
1. **What the keyboard sends**: `cargo run -p varimu-app --example midi_ports` echoes a
   keyboard's messages; check whether the user's ("masterkey 61") sends note-off velocity. If
   not, the felt's approach speed is one provisional value and the package is smaller.
2. **The reference** (`varimu/rhodes/key.py` has the hammer's contact, `key.contact`, to copy
   the structure from): the felt as a one-sided lossy spring at the damper's point
   (`fits["damper_x_m"]`), rising at the release's speed to its rest position, solved inside
   the step with the free system's instantaneous response. While the tine's swing exceeds the
   felt's compression the tine leaves and re-meets it; once it stays in contact, switch to the
   compiled damped system as today (so the cost is only the first milliseconds of a release).
3. **Measure** on keys 8, 25, 43, 61, 78 at three amplitudes and three release speeds: the
   time to -60 dB, and the release's spectrum against the switch's. If no source for a real
   release exists (jRhodes3d has no releases; the Suitcase mp3's chord releases are the only
   reference here), every new value is provisional and bounded.
4. **A/B** against the switch, loudness-matched. **Stop: the user's call** whether it is heard.
5. If yes: the port in `harp.rs` (a second contact kind beside the hammer's), release velocity
   through `media::NoteEvent::Off` (it carries none today: check), agreement tests as RH3's.
6. Optional, after: the pedal as a position (CC 64's value lifting the rail: the felt's
   preload), for half-pedalling.

**Done when** the reference's release is measured against the switch, the user has chosen, and
(if adopted) Rust agrees with the reference within a tested bound and the live load with every
key released at once is reported.

## SC4. Attack and sustain forensics (measurement only)

**Why.** Three packages (SC5, SC7, and the "hammer's tick" option in RHODES_PLAN.md) depend on
what actually reaches the pickups, and nobody has looked. jRhodes3d was recorded at the harp
connector, so whatever is in its first milliseconds came through the pickups: no microphone,
no room. RHODES_VARIATION.md judged action noise "barely at the pickups" without a measurement;
RHODES_PLAN.md finds the harp 7-14 dB short at 4-16 kHz and resolved the attack only as a time
to peak [docs].

**Steps** (`scripts/rhodes_attack_forensics.py` -> `reports/rhodes/attack/`, reusing
`rhodes_vs_jrhodes.py`'s loading, `from_onset` and `harp_note`), per note and layer, real
against ours at the layer's centre velocity:
1. **The thump**: energy below 0.7 f0 in the first 50 ms, re the note's peak. A hammer's
   reaction through a compliant rail would show here.
2. **The tick**: energy above 4 kHz in the first 10 ms that is not at a harmonic of f0 or at
   the bell mode's sidebands, re the peak.
3. **Beating**: the fundamental's band-pass envelope (as `decay_db_s`) minus its straight-line
   fit in dB, from 0.3 s on: the residual's rms and its strongest modulation rate. Two
   polarizations slightly detuned give a slow beat; ours has one plane and should read ~0.
4. **Two-stage decay**: the slope over 0.3-2 s against 2-8 s.
5. Mind the caveats: the samples are EQ'd (a fixed EQ changes levels, not whether a component
   exists), noise-reduced (low-level broadband noise may have been removed: a missing tick in
   the soft layers proves nothing), 16-bit.

**Done when** `reports/rhodes/attack/README.md` has a table per hypothesis (thump, tick, beats,
two-stage decay: present or not, how large, in which register) and a recommendation for SC5,
SC7 and the tick. A few minutes of compute.

## SC5. The rail: thump and sympathetic ring (gated on SC4)

**Only if SC4 finds a thump or the user asks for the pedal-down halo.** Today the tone
generators hang on a rigid rail: a pedal-down chord is the exact sum of independent notes, and
a sleeping key starts from exactly zero [code: `harp.rs:738`]. One mechanism would give the
thump, the sympathetic ring and "tines never at rest" (RHODES_VARIATION.md item 2): the rail
as a compliant body with a few modes, driven by every key's mount force and shaking every
key's base.

**A design sketch, not steps** (engine work: ask the user before starting): the voices are
independent and threaded, rendered ahead in chunks of 256 steps. A rail coupled one chunk late
(2.7 ms at 96 kHz) keeps that structure: each chunk, sum the awake voices' mount forces into
the rail's modes, and feed the rail's motion back as a base excitation of every voice in the
next chunk. The struck key's own thump comes from its own voice within the chunk. No source
gives the rail's modes: they would be provisional and bounded, or fitted to SC4's thump. The
Clavinet's `varimu/clavinet/knock.py` is the nearest precedent.

## SC6. Bass tines at ff (measurement, then options)

**The question.** With the 2026-11-07 velocity law the bass tines swing 10-14 mm at ff [docs].
The manual gives the lifted damper's clearance as 3/8-1/2 in, 9.5-12.7 mm [code:
`mk1.yaml:58`], and RH2 took the tone bar to be ~9.5 mm above the tine (provisional) [docs].
The model has no collision: the tine passes through both. It also has no magnet pull and no
amplitude-dependent pitch, where the real F1's loudest layer is 20 cents sharp of its softest
[docs]. And the fit's worst keys are the low bass, too rich at soft velocities [docs]. These may
be one problem.

**Steps** (`scripts/rhodes_bass_clearance.py`, the Python reference `key.strike`):
1. Keys 8-30 at velocities 64 / 100 / 127: the tine's peak displacement at its tip, at the
   damper's point and along the bar's length, against the two clearances.
2. If the model's tine crosses a clearance: say at which velocity per key, and what a real tine
   would meet there (the lifted felt first).
3. The velocity law was fitted on keys 20-76's median [code: `mk1.yaml:69`]: report the bass
   keys' own fitted speeds if the scratch fit can be re-run, against the one law.
4. **Options to the user**, with numbers: (a) a per-register velocity law (the bass hammers
   slower than the one line); (b) the lifted felt as a soft stop, SC3's contact at another
   clearance; (c) the magnet's pull as a force (RH5), which also gives the pitch glide;
   (d) a larger gap in the bass, as the manual allows (RHODES_PLAN.md's first candidate).

**Done when** the table and the options are in this file. Minutes of compute.

## SC7. Repeated notes: the second polarization (gated on SC4)

**Only if SC4 finds beating, or the user still hears repeated notes as mechanical after SC2 and
SC3.** A strike at the same velocity on a resting key is the same computation every time
[docs: RHODES_VARIATION.md]. The physical sources of variation are the tine's second plane (a
hammer a fraction of a millimetre off centre excites it differently each strike; the planes'
slight detuning beats) and residual motion (SC5).

**A design sketch** (RH5's, the largest job here): the beam FE in both planes (each key's modes
about doubled: the harp alone goes from 0.22x to ~0.45x real time with every key ringing
[estimate]); a flux map over the tip's sideways position too (`pickup3d.py` can compute it; the
compiled spline becomes three-dimensional); a strike's sideways offset drawn per strike from
`(seed, key, strike index)` so a take replays bit for bit; its spread provisional and bounded.
Ask the user before starting.

## SC8. The noise floor

**The gap.** Between notes the Suitcase is digital silence: the files have no noise block, and
the builder has no noise for op-amps, BJTs or diodes [code]. A real preamp has a floor, and
what sits ahead of the panning network is panned with the vibrato [memory].

**Whether it is audible is not obvious** [estimate]: A1A's 10 kOhm input resistor is 12.8
nV/rtHz, the harp's 1425 ohm 4.9; with an op-amp of ~20-30 nV/rtHz [memory: read the MC1458's
sheet, `data/datasheets/opamps/README.md`] that is ~25-35 nV/rtHz at the input, ~4-5 uV over
20 kHz, x22 = ~0.1 mV at A1A's output against ~1 V for a loud note: about -80 dB re forte,
-55 to -60 dB re a soft note. Measure before building anything elaborate.

**Steps.**
1. The op-amps' voltage noise as input resistors, `clean_pre.yaml`'s way (`RN`,
   `tolerance: none`, a `derived:` source from the sheet's figure), on A1A, A1B, A3A, A3B in
   `rhodes_suitcase.yaml`. A resistor in series with an input changes nothing else only if the
   input draws no current: check against the macro's `r_in_ohm` (2 MOhm) and say what moved.
2. A `noise: {seed: 1}` block. **Careful:** `build(..., noise=None)` then turns noise on for
   every Python render of the circuit. Existing studies and tests (`suitcase_study.py`,
   `tests/test_rhodes_suitcase.py`, `test_rhodes_amp.py`, the crackle test's bounds) must pass
   `noise=False` or be re-checked; the fixtures are re-exported. The app's Play view already
   has a "Noise" switch for devices whose file has the block, off by default.
3. Regenerate the 100 W file (`make_rhodes_suitcase_100w.py`).
4. **Measure** (the preamp-only circuit, production, ~4x real time): the idle noise at `out` /
   `out_r` in dBFS and dB re a forte and a soft note; with the vibrato on, the noise's own
   left / right swing; the live load with Noise on (`examples/play.rs`).
5. Not in the model and left out: hum picked up by the harp and the cable (no source, no stray
   field model), the power amp's BJT noise.

**Done when** the numbers are in this file and the user has heard a soft passage with and
without. If the floor is below ~-70 dB re a soft note, record "inaudible" and stop.

# The tube synth

It is an invented instrument, so "realistic" means: what a careful build of this circuit from
real parts would do. The circuit is the reference; the gaps are where the parts or the player
are idealized.

## TS1. The reactor's magnetics (measurement, then options)

**The gap.** The reactor's cores are the pitch and the cutoff, and they rest on two things
nobody has measured: mumetall's incremental permeability under DC bias is checked only against
Gans' law (an estimate), within x0.59-1.95 over the range used; and Jiles-Atherton minor loops
ratchet where real nickel-iron loops close within a few cycles [docs: TUBE_SYNTH_PLAN.md S1].
So the settle after every key (17-45 ms, big drops 0.1-0.24 s) is partly a model artifact.
SYNTH_MASTER_PLAN.md decides Preisach cores for the `master` quality only (its Q6), which
would make a render settle differently from what was played. A Preisach core would also have
return-point memory: where the pitch lands depends on the direction a note was approached
from. How large that is, nobody knows.

**Steps (Python only, no engine or device edits).**
1. `varimu/synth/preisach_core.py` (outside the render cache key): a scalar core on
   `varimu/devices/preisach.py`'s ensemble (read `PreisachParams` and `Preisach` first, and
   `varimu/devices/tape_fit.py` for how the tape's was fitted), fitted to the same targets as
   the JA mumetall in `config/magnetics.yaml`: the four datasheet scalars **and** the Rayleigh
   targets (CLAUDE.md: the scalars alone gave a bad fit once).
2. `scripts/reactor_preisach_study.py` -> `reports/tube_synth/preisach/`, repeating
   `scripts/reactor_material_study.py`'s experiments on the core alone, JA beside Preisach:
   - incremental permeability against bias, 0-100 A/m, at 0.5 and 5 mT pp, with Gans' law;
   - cycles for a minor loop to close after a bias step (JA: tens to hundreds [docs]);
   - **return-point memory as pitch**: the incremental permeability at the same bias reached
     from below and from above; with f proportional to 1 / sqrt(L), the difference in cents is
     600 log2 of the ratio. Do this at the biases of low, middle and high notes.
   - the cost per step relative to JA in Python (a rough guide to the Rust cost).
3. **Options to the user**, with the numbers: (a) Q6 as planned (master only); (b) a Preisach
   core device for the live qualities too, so the played and rendered instruments agree (a new
   device kind in `varimu/devices` and circuitsim with an agreement test, the tuning tables
   re-measured: a large package, and an edit inside the render cache key); (c) leave JA and
   record the size of the artifact.

**Honest limits.** Preisach closes a loop at once; real material accommodates over a few
cycles [memory], so it idealizes the other way. The only real answer is a measurement (a
toroid with a control winding through the audio interface: the user deferred bench work), or
IEEE 1464206, which is paywalled [docs].

**Done when** the study's report and the options are in this file. Minutes of compute.

## TS2. Mismatch: every part is nominal

**The gap.** No part deviates from its value and every tube is exactly its fitted model. So
every cancellation is perfect: the two reactor cores are identical (odd harmonics 134 dB under
the second in the control circuit [docs]), the push-pull VCA's halves match (thump 1.3 mV, hum
-71 dBFS [docs]), the ring modulator's four selenium cells and transformer halves match. A
built unit leaks at each of these, and for a period ring modulator the carrier leak is part of
its sound [memory]. SYNTH_IDEAS.md #17 and SYNTH_MASTER_PLAN.md Q7 plan unit draws; neither
measured what they would do.

**Step 1: measure first, Python only, no builder work.** `scripts/synth_mismatch_study.py` ->
`reports/tube_synth/mismatch/`. Build scratch copies of the circuit in memory (load the spec,
override values through `spec.resolve(s, {...})`; where two parts share one value key, e.g.
the two cores' turns, a scratch YAML with separate keys) with one mismatch at a time and all
together, at sourced sizes:

| Mismatch | Where | A realistic size (find the source) |
|---|---|---|
| The two reactor cores (filter TA / TB, oscillator OTA / OTB) | turns or core area | a few percent [memory] |
| The VCA's 6386 sections | the device's emission / mu (`device_params`, as the 670's `v6386_{a,b}.*`) | the 670 genome's bounds |
| The output and interstage transformers' halves | turns | ~1% [memory] |
| The ring's selenium cells | the cell model's saturation current / series resistance | selenium's spread: find a sheet |
| Resistors, capacitors | by class | 5-10% / 10-20% of the period [memory] |

Metrics, each against the nominal circuit (the note-playing pattern is `test_tube_synth.py`'s
`run`; the ring's and the envelope's measurements are in `ring_study.py` and `adsr_study.py`):
the gate's thump with silence in (mV); hum with a key held (dBFS, 60 / 120 Hz); the oscillator's
bleed with the gate closed; the fundamental in the reactor's control circuit; the thump of a
fast cutoff step; the ring's carrier leak with one input silent (dB re its output); the pitch's
offset (the tuning tables absorb a static one).

**Step 2 (only if step 1 finds something audible; a decision is needed first).** Units in the
app need the draws in Rust. Python's draws are numpy's `default_rng` (SeedSequence, PCG64 and
numpy's sampling) [code: `parts.py:1057`]; porting that bit for bit is real work. The
alternative, drawing through the project's Philox streams as the Clavinet's wear does, would
change every existing unit of the 670 (Phase 5's pool) unless it is opt-in per circuit. **That
choice is the user's**: write both with their consequences. Then: a `tolerance:` block in
`tube_synth.yaml` with spread 0 by default (the IR unchanged, the goldens without a diff), a
UNIT number on the panel, and tuning tables per unit (`vco_study.py --tuning`,
`saw_884_tuning.py`), which SYNTH_IDEAS.md #17 already notes.

**Done when** step 1's table is in this file with a recommendation. Each run is a 1-2 s note
at draft, ~0.9x real time: the whole study is minutes.

## TS3. Temperature beyond the cathodes (measurement, then options)

**The gap.** Only the cathodes have a temperature (one for all tubes, a 7 s lag). Forty
seconds after a cold start the pitch is within half a cent, and from then on the only slow
drift is the mains wander, 1.8 cents rms [docs]. "Drift as character" (the plan's decision 2)
is mostly absent once warm. A real chassis warms for tens of minutes, and a reactor-tuned
oscillator would follow its cores' permeability, its tank capacitors, its resistors and its
5651 references as they warm [memory]. SYNTH_MASTER_PLAN.md Q7 plans a chassis warm-up at
master only; COMPONENT_DEPTH_PLAN.md lists tempco among its part models (nothing built).

**Steps (Python only).**
1. **Sensitivities** (`scripts/synth_thermal_study.py` -> `reports/tube_synth/thermal/`): the
   held pitch (cents) and the cutoff per +1% of each class of value, one at a time: the tank
   capacitors (expect ~-8.6 cents per percent: f proportional to 1 / sqrt(C)), the core
   material's permeability (a scratch material entry; do not edit `config/magnetics.yaml`), the
   5651s' operating voltage, the cathode and plate resistors, the supply's resistors.
2. **Sourced tempcos**: mumetall's permeability against temperature (Vacuumschmelze's or
   Carpenter's data), paper / mica / ceramic capacitors of the period, carbon composition
   resistors, the 5651's sheet. Each with its source; where none is found, say so and bound it.
3. **The prediction**: sensitivities x tempcos x an assumed chassis rise (state it and its
   source; it is the weakest number) = the drift over a warm-up and per kelvin of room
   temperature, per mechanism.
4. **Options to the user**: (a) nothing (if the sum is a few cents); (b) a chassis temperature
   as a slow state at master (Q7); (c) the same live. For (c) note how values would move in a
   running stream: `Transient::update` rebuilds and restarts at first order, so an update every
   few seconds must be checked for clicks (`tests/stream.rs` has update tests).

**Done when** the table (mechanism, sensitivity, tempco and source, predicted drift) and the
options are in this file. Minutes of compute; the sources are the work.

## TS4. The keyboard as a circuit (measurement, then options)

**The gap.** The player's interface is outside the physics: gate and pitch are ideal sources
that step within a sample, the app holds the last key's pitch forever, key priority is
software, and a contact never bounces [code]. A period keyboard is contacts on a resistor
string: contacts chatter for a few milliseconds [memory], a keyboard without logic has low- or
high-note priority rather than last-note, and with no key down nothing drives the pitch node,
so it droops through whatever leaks.

**It is the lowest-value synth package**: the oscillators run free, so attacks already differ
from note to note, and the keyboard is invented either way.

**Steps (Python only: the controls are arrays, so no circuit edit is needed).**
1. **Bounce**: the `gate` array with a chatter pattern at key-down (a few make-and-break
   cycles over 1-5 ms; find a source for leaf or wire contacts), at the fastest ATTACK and a
   slow one: the envelope's and the output's first 20 ms against a clean step, over ten seeded
   patterns. Is the spread audible beside the oscillator's own phase?
2. **Release droop**: a scratch copy where the pitch string disconnects at key-up (a `contact`
   device, `KEY_CONTACT@1`, in series with RPSUM, worked by the gate) and the 1 GOhm numerical
   grid leak is replaced by a sourced leakage (the 6V6GT's grid current at its bias, the glide
   capacitor's leakage): the pitch over a long RELEASE.
3. **Options to the user**: bounce as a seeded pattern in the `Performer` (drawn from the
   performance's seed and the event's index, so a take replays bit for bit); the pitch contact
   in the circuit; priority modes (low / high / last) on the Play panel.

**Done when** the two measurements and the options are in this file.
