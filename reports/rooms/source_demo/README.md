# A combo amp facing somewhere: a listening demo

Generated by `scripts/room_source_demo.py` (docs/ROOM_LOOP_PLAN.md, "Status (2026-11-05)":
directional sources). An electric guitar (David Tyo's "Long Way Home", Cambridge-MT
multitrack: educational use, never committed) through a 1x12 combo, modelled as a 15 cm cone on
a 25 cm sphere (`varimu/rooms/directivity.py` `amp_1x12`), its cone 40 cm off the floor in the
live room (7 x 5.5 x 3.2 m, furnished), 95 dB SPL on its axis at 1 m. A spaced pair of clean
small omnis 2.6 m away. The wave solver below 500 Hz with the cabinet in its grid.

| Folder | The amp |
|---|---|
| `toward/` | faces the pair |
| `away/` | faces the wall behind it, 1.4 m back |
| `omni/` | the same source as an omni (every source before directivity) |

Each `room_pair.wav` is at -18 LUFS, so what differs is the timbre and the room, not the level.
The raw levels (`levels.json`): omni -27.4 LUFS, toward -30.2, away -31.3: a cabinet of the
same on-axis level puts less power into the room than an omni (it beams), and at 2.6 m in a
live room facing away costs only ~1 dB more: the pair hears mostly the room either way.

**Listen for:** `toward` against `away`: the cone's top end (the 12-inch's beaming above ~2 kHz)
direct in `toward`, gone to the room in `away` (darker, more distant, the cabinet's back). Then
`omni` against both: the omni's top end reaches the room in every direction, so its room sound
is brighter than either real-amp version.
